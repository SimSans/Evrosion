# Evrosion R-002 — Founder Stewardship, Foundation Governance & Succession

**Status:** ACCEPTED DESIGN DIRECTION  
**Series:** R — Release, Project & Sustainability  
**Decision:** R-002  
**Purpose:** Define who controls Evrosion during the founder era, how the commercial company and future Foundation are separated, how succession works, and how Evrosion avoids capture after the founder is gone.

> **Important:** This document freezes governance intent, not jurisdiction-specific legal instruments. Exact company, foundation, trust, share-class, inheritance, tax, and nonprofit structures must later be translated into enforceable documents by qualified counsel in the chosen jurisdiction.

---

# 1. Core Principle

> **Founder control while the founder actively leads. Distributed constitutional stewardship after the founder era.**

Evrosion deliberately uses two different governance models for two different eras.

## Founder era

While the founder is alive, capable, and actively leading Evrosion:

- the founder has final authority over Evrosion;
- investors cannot remove or overrule the founder;
- a future Foundation does not possess authority to override the founder;
- emergency override authority remains with the founder;
- major project, technical, business, and governance decisions ultimately remain under founder control.

## Post-founder era

After the founder voluntarily retires, dies, or becomes permanently unable to lead through a narrowly defined independent process:

- no successor inherits founder-level absolute authority;
- constitutional/project stewardship transfers to the Evrosion Foundation;
- commercial day-to-day management remains delegated to professional executive leadership;
- users receive formal participation in major constitutional decisions;
- economic ownership and governance authority are deliberately separated.

---

# 2. Why Evrosion Uses a Dual Structure

Evrosion should eventually distinguish between:

## Evrosion Systems

The commercial company.

It may handle:

- OEM agreements;
- enterprise services;
- marketplace operations;
- optional AI/cloud services;
- employees;
- contractors;
- commercial engineering;
- support;
- certification services;
- commercial licensing;
- investment;
- revenue-generating operations.

## Evrosion Foundation

A later public-purpose/stewardship entity.

It exists to protect:

- the Evrosion project identity;
- long-term open-source stewardship;
- constitutional principles;
- official certification authority;
- trademark/project continuity;
- post-founder governance;
- the separation between commercial ownership and project sovereignty.

The Foundation is not intended to micromanage ordinary company operations.

---

# 3. Foundation Creation Is Deferred

The Evrosion Foundation does **not** need to exist immediately.

Early Evrosion should remain operationally simple.

Initial model:

```text
Founder
   │
   └── Evrosion Systems / early project structure
```

The Foundation should be created only when Evrosion has enough:

- users;
- contributors;
- revenue;
- commercial relationships;
- trademarks/assets;
- public significance;

to justify a separate stewardship entity.

The plan is defined now so that future growth does not force rushed governance decisions.

---

# 4. Initial Ownership

At the beginning:

- the founder owns/controls the Evrosion name and project identity;
- the founder controls the commercial company;
- the founder controls project direction;
- the founder selects the initial Foundation board when the Foundation is eventually created.

Transfer of specific assets to the Foundation is deferred.

Possible future Foundation-controlled assets include:

- Evrosion trademarks;
- official certification marks;
- constitutional/governance documents;
- official project identity;
- release identity/signing policy;
- selected community infrastructure.

Exact asset allocation is intentionally not frozen by R-002.

---

# 5. Founder Authority

While the founder is active:

> **Final authority remains with the founder.**

This includes authority over:

- technical direction;
- architecture;
- Foundation appointments;
- company strategy;
- licensing strategy;
- business strategy;
- emergency action;
- governance reform;
- commercial relationships.

A Foundation created during the founder era exists as a separate stewardship structure but may not override the active founder.

Investors may receive economic participation but not founder-level governance authority.

---

# 6. When the Founder Era Ends

The phrase **active founder** must not be vague.

Founder control ends only through one of the following:

1. **voluntary retirement or resignation explicitly initiated by the founder;**
2. **death;**
3. **permanent incapacity established under a pre-defined independent legal/medical process.**

A Foundation board, investor group, executive team, or shareholder majority must not be able to simply vote that the founder is "inactive."

The incapacity process must be designed so that it cannot be manufactured as a governance coup.

Exact legal implementation is deferred, but should involve independent evidence and protections appropriate to the chosen jurisdiction.

---

# 7. Successor Selection

Succession uses a hybrid model.

## Preferred path

The founder may designate or nominate a successor.

## Fallback

If no valid successor exists when succession is required, the Evrosion Foundation selects the successor under its post-founder governance rules.

The successor does **not** inherit the founder's absolute authority.

The successor becomes a leader within the post-founder governance system.

---

# 8. No Second Founder

The founder's authority is unique to the founding era.

After succession:

- no CEO;
- no Foundation chair;
- no heir;
- no investor;
- no maintainer;
- no single individual;

may acquire the founder's former absolute control merely by appointment or inheritance.

This is a hard R-002 rule.

---

# 9. Post-Founder Foundation Board

After the founder era, the Foundation is governed by a small mixed board.

Target:

```text
5–7 members preferred
10 members maximum
```

The board should contain complementary roles rather than a single constituency.

Possible seats may include:

- core technical/architecture expertise;
- security expertise;
- community representation;
- contributor/maintainer representation;
- Evrosion Systems representation;
- legal/business/governance expertise;
- another independent specialist role where needed.

The exact seat map may evolve.

The board must never become dominated by only:

- Evrosion Systems;
- investors;
- maintainers;
- community managers;
- one employer;
- one commercial partner.

---

# 10. Board Selection Is Intentionally Deferred

The initial Foundation board is expected to be handpicked by the founder.

After the founder era, the permanent mechanism for selecting board members is intentionally left open until Evrosion has a real ecosystem from which to design it.

Potential mechanisms may later include:

- user elections;
- contributor elections;
- board-appointed specialist seats;
- company-nominated seats;
- mixed nomination systems.

R-002 currently freezes only this principle:

> **No single constituency may control board formation by itself.**

---

# 11. Board Capture Protections

The future detailed bylaws should include protections such as:

- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- mandatory recusal where appropriate;
- staggered terms;
- vacancy rules;
- quorum rules;
- removal-for-cause processes;
- limits on one organization/employer holding multiple seats;
- transparent minutes for non-sensitive governance decisions;
- defined emergency succession for board vacancies.

Exact implementation remains future work.

---

# 12. Users Have Formal Governance Rights

After the founder era, major constitutional decisions must not be made by the Foundation board alone.

Eligible Evrosion users receive formal voting rights for protected decisions.

This is not ordinary product polling.

It is part of post-founder constitutional governance.

---

# 13. Eligible User Voting

Voting eligibility must be resistant to bot/Sybil capture.

Eligibility may be based on meaningful participation signals such as:

- sustained Evrosion usage;
- long-term project participation;
- contributor history;
- maintainer history;
- certified-device ownership;
- meaningful community participation;
- other legitimate participation indicators.

Using Layer 3 may be one possible relevance signal for particular questions, but must not be a universal requirement for citizenship in the project.

---

# 14. Anti-Sybil Transparency Rule

Evrosion should not publish every exact anti-abuse threshold, because doing so would make automated gaming easier.

However, **the governance system itself must not become an opaque black box.**

Therefore:

## Public

- voter classes;
- categories of participation;
- broad weighting principles;
- permitted weight ranges;
- decision-specific relevance rules;
- appeal/audit mechanisms.

## May remain confidential

- exact bot-detection heuristics;
- abuse thresholds;
- fraud signals;
- rate limits;
- adversarial detection methods.

The anti-abuse mechanism may be opaque.

**The political/governance outcome logic may not be secretly adjustable.**

---

# 15. Context-Dependent Vote Weighting

Eligible voters do not necessarily carry identical weight in every decision.

Vote weighting may depend on the subject being decided.

Examples:

## Kernel/security issue

Greater relevance may attach to:

- kernel maintainers;
- security experts;
- affected technical users.

## Accessibility/UX issue

Greater relevance may attach to:

- affected users;
- accessibility specialists;
- relevant maintainers.

## Community policy

Greater relevance may attach to:

- long-term users;
- contributors;
- community participants.

## Constitutional change

The electorate should be broad, with weighting designed to avoid one specialist group monopolizing the decision.

---

# 16. Voting Rules Must Be Frozen Before a Ballot

A future Foundation must not be able to manipulate a vote by changing weights after seeing likely outcomes.

For every major vote:

1. eligibility rules are fixed before voting opens;
2. permitted weighting rules are fixed before voting opens;
3. relevant electorate classes are published;
4. no weights may be changed during the ballot;
5. the result should be auditable;
6. disputes should have a defined review path.

This is a direct anti-capture requirement.

---

# 17. Evrosion Employees Have No Automatic Voting Privilege

Working for Evrosion Systems does not grant extra user-voting authority.

Employees and executives participate under the same relevance criteria as other eligible users.

An employee may carry greater weight where they possess legitimately relevant expertise.

That weight comes from relevance/expertise, **not employment status**.

---

# 18. Founder-Era Investment

If Evrosion Systems ever accepts outside equity during the founder era:

- the founder retains controlling authority;
- investors cannot remove the active founder;
- investors cannot outvote the active founder;
- investor ownership should initially be limited to roughly **20% maximum total** unless the founder deliberately changes that strategy.

The exact share-class implementation is deferred.

Possible tools include:

- multiple-vote founder shares;
- non-voting or limited-voting economic shares;
- other control-enhancing structures allowed by the chosen jurisdiction.

Current EU law explicitly recognizes multiple-vote shares as a control-enhancing mechanism that can allow controlling shareholders to raise capital while retaining decision-making power, subject to national implementation and safeguards.

---

# 19. Founder-Era Equity Cap Is Not Eternal

The approximately 20% outside-equity ceiling is a founder-era strategic preference, not a permanent constitutional limit.

After the founder era:

- equity percentages may increase;
- decrease;
- be eliminated;
- become widely dispersed;
- or remain concentrated.

The permanent rule is different:

> **Economic ownership may change. Constitutional governance remains with the Foundation.**

---

# 20. Founder Shares and Family Inheritance

On the founder's death or permanent departure, the founder intends the economic value of their Evrosion Systems ownership to pass to family:

- spouse;
- children;
- other designated family beneficiaries;

according to estate planning established by the founder.

The exact division is an inheritance/estate-planning matter.

However:

> **Founder-level governance rights do not pass through inheritance.**

Family inherits economic value.

Family does not automatically inherit sovereign project control.

---

# 21. Economic Rights Must Remain Real

Separating governance from economics must not make inherited shares fake or confiscatory.

Family/investor economic rights should remain clearly defined under the future legal structure, including where applicable:

- dividends;
- sale rights;
- transfer rights;
- economic participation;
- liquidation rights.

The Foundation's governance authority must not become an excuse to arbitrarily confiscate or nullify legitimate economic rights.

Exact protections depend on company law and future financing structure.

---

# 22. The Critical Governance/Economic Split

After the founder era, Evrosion must legally separate:

```text
ECONOMIC OWNERSHIP
```

from:

```text
PROJECT / GOVERNANCE CONTROL
```

This cannot exist only as a promise.

The Foundation must possess an enforceable governance mechanism sufficient to preserve its role even if economic shares are sold.

Possible future legal implementations include:

- controlling voting shares;
- special governance shares;
- veto/guardian shares;
- Foundation nomination rights;
- ownership through a steward entity;
- other jurisdiction-specific control structures.

The contract to freeze now is:

> **Economic shareholders must not be able to vote the Foundation's constitutional authority out of existence.**

---

# 23. Why This Matters

Without a protected governance mechanism, the following design would fail:

```text
Family owns company shares
Foundation appoints CEO
```

because ordinary shareholders could potentially change the company board or governance structure and bypass the Foundation.

Therefore post-founder governance must be legally anchored.

There are real precedents for separating capital ownership from voting control.

For example, the Carlsberg Foundation currently controls a majority of the voting rights in Carlsberg A/S while holding a materially smaller share of the company's capital. Its charter specifically preserves majority voting control while allowing the company to raise outside equity.

Evrosion does not need to copy Carlsberg's legal structure, but the precedent demonstrates the underlying governance concept.

---

# 24. CEO After the Founder Era

Evrosion Systems should still have a strong CEO after the founder era.

The CEO runs normal commercial operations, including:

- hiring;
- budgets;
- pricing;
- products;
- contracts;
- enterprise services;
- OEM relationships;
- marketplace operations;
- operational strategy.

The Foundation should not become a committee that approves routine business decisions.

---

# 25. CEO Appointment and Removal

After the founder era:

> **The Evrosion Foundation appoints and removes the CEO of Evrosion Systems through its protected governance rights.**

Family shareholders do not control CEO appointment merely because they inherited economic shares.

Investors do not acquire CEO appointment authority merely by purchasing economic shares.

The exact corporate mechanism is deferred.

---

# 26. CEO Is Powerful but Not Sovereign

The post-founder CEO may run the company aggressively and efficiently.

The CEO cannot unilaterally:

- close the Evrosion core;
- abolish Layer 3;
- introduce mandatory telemetry into official Evrosion;
- transfer the project trademark;
- seize the Foundation;
- rewrite the Evrosion Constitution;
- override user constitutional votes;
- eliminate Foundation governance.

The CEO is an executive, not a second founder.

---

# 27. Trademark and Project Identity

Initially, the founder owns/controls the Evrosion trademark/project identity.

Transfer to the Foundation may occur later when the Foundation exists and the project has matured.

The eventual goal is to prevent the commercial company from being able to take the official Evrosion identity hostage.

Open-source foundations commonly treat neutral trademark ownership as a way to prevent one commercial participant from controlling the project's identity.

The exact timing and transfer mechanism remain deferred.

---

# 28. Trademark / Core Identity Transfer After the Founder Era

Once the Foundation is the project steward, selling or transferring the Evrosion trademark/core official identity is permitted only in an extraordinary case.

Required direction:

```text
100% Foundation board approval
+
overwhelming supermajority of eligible users
```

The exact user percentage is deferred, but must be intentionally extreme.

This is the highest-protection class of governance action.

---

# 29. Constitutional Principles

Protected principles include, at minimum, major commitments such as:

- open-source core;
- owner sovereignty;
- Layer 3 / Absolute Access;
- no mandatory telemetry;
- free essential security updates during supported lifetimes;
- no forced-account dependency for basic ownership;
- capability-first authority and other architectural laws formally designated constitutional.

The exact constitutional register may evolve.

---

# 30. Constitutional Amendment Threshold

Protected constitutional principles may be amended after the founder era, but only through very high consensus.

Required direction:

```text
large Foundation-board supermajority
+
large eligible-user supermajority
```

This threshold is intentionally lower than transferring the Evrosion trademark/project identity, but still difficult.

Indicative future ranges such as 75–80% board and 70–80% eligible-user approval have been discussed, but are **not locked** by R-002.

---

# 31. Foundation Self-Amendment

A future Foundation must not be able to casually rewrite its own rules to make itself unaccountable.

Changes to:

- Foundation governance;
- user voting rights;
- protected board structure;
- constitutional thresholds;
- Foundation/company control boundaries;

require a large Foundation-board supermajority plus a large eligible-user supermajority.

Exact percentages are deferred.

---

# 32. Emergency Powers

After the founder era, the Foundation possesses narrow emergency authority for events such as:

- catastrophic security compromise;
- release-signing-key compromise;
- malicious update;
- critical infrastructure breach;
- major outage;
- active supply-chain compromise;
- comparable existential operational emergencies.

Emergency powers exist so Evrosion does not need to wait for a lengthy governance ballot while systems are actively failing.

---

# 33. Emergency Powers Are Temporary

Post-founder emergency actions must:

- address a defined emergency;
- be narrowly scoped;
- be logged and reviewable;
- expire automatically;
- undergo retrospective review;
- require normal governance approval to become permanent policy.

Emergency authority may suspend or protect systems necessary for containment and recovery.

---

# 34. Things Emergency Powers Cannot Do

Post-founder emergency authority must **not** be usable to:

- permanently amend the Evrosion Constitution;
- sell/transfer the Evrosion trademark;
- abolish user voting;
- permanently expand Foundation powers;
- transfer project ownership;
- permanently close the core;
- convert a temporary emergency restriction into a permanent governance change without normal approval.

This prevents "permanent emergency" capture.

---

# 35. Founder-Era Emergency Authority

While the founder remains active:

> **Founder authority remains final, including emergency authority.**

The post-founder emergency limitations govern the Foundation after founder control has ended.

---

# 36. Separation of Money and Technical Legitimacy

Financial support must not automatically buy governance power.

An OEM, investor, sponsor, or enterprise customer may pay Evrosion Systems substantial sums.

That payment does not automatically grant:

- technical votes;
- Foundation seats;
- constitutional authority;
- project-direction vetoes.

This continues R-001's rule:

> **Funding purchases economic value and services, not architectural authority.**

---

# 37. Governance Must Be Auditable

Post-founder Foundation governance should be understandable to the community.

Subject to legitimate needs for:

- security confidentiality;
- personnel privacy;
- legal privilege;
- commercial confidentiality;

major governance decisions should publish:

- what was decided;
- which governance threshold applied;
- board vote totals;
- user vote totals;
- relevant conflicts/recusals;
- whether emergency authority was used.

The project must not become governed by invisible private arrangements.

---

# 39. Adversarial Review Findings

The R-002 adversarial pass identified several failure modes and addressed them.

## Failure: shareholders bypass the Foundation

**Risk:** Family/investors own shares and simply vote away Foundation authority.

**Resolution:** Post-founder Foundation governance rights must be legally anchored independently from ordinary economic ownership.

---

## Failure: future CEO becomes "Founder 2"

**Risk:** Successor CEO accumulates founder-level control.

**Resolution:** Founder authority is explicitly non-transferable. CEO controls operations only.

---

## Failure: board declares founder inactive

**Risk:** Foundation/investors manufacture a succession event.

**Resolution:** Founder era ends only through explicit retirement, death, or tightly defined independent incapacity determination.

---

## Failure: opaque user-vote weighting

**Risk:** Future Foundation silently changes weights to manufacture an outcome.

**Resolution:** Governance categories and weight ranges are public/auditable; anti-bot detection can remain confidential; rules freeze before each ballot.

---

## Failure: emergency dictatorship

**Risk:** A board uses a security emergency to permanently rewrite governance.

**Resolution:** Emergency powers are temporary and cannot amend constitution/trademark/control rules permanently.

---

## Failure: family inheritance accidentally transfers project control

**Risk:** A nontechnical heir becomes Evrosion sovereign by inheritance.

**Resolution:** Family inherits economics, not founder governance.

---

## Failure: Foundation destroys shareholder economics

**Risk:** Governance separation leaves heirs/investors with meaningless shares.

**Resolution:** Legitimate economic rights remain protected; Foundation governance is not confiscatory authority.

---

## Failure: one constituency captures the Foundation

**Risk:** Company, maintainers, investors, or community faction dominates.

**Resolution:** Small mixed board; no single constituency may dominate; board-selection mechanism later must preserve this property.

---

## Failure: trademark can be sold quietly

**Risk:** Future board transfers Evrosion identity to an acquirer.

**Resolution:** Unanimous board approval plus overwhelming eligible-user supermajority.

---

## Failure: Foundation rewrites its own safeguards

**Risk:** Board lowers voting thresholds and then captures the project.

**Resolution:** Governance self-amendment itself requires high board and user supermajorities.

---

# 40. External Precedents Supporting the Direction

R-002 does not copy any existing organization exactly.

Several real structures demonstrate parts of the model.

## Mozilla

Mozilla uses a nonprofit Foundation plus a taxable Mozilla Corporation, with the Corporation serving the Foundation's public-purpose mission.

This demonstrates practical separation between public-purpose stewardship and commercial/product activity.

## Linux Foundation / open-source trademarks

The Linux Foundation argues that neutral control of project trademarks prevents a single commercial participant from holding the community's identity hostage.

This supports Evrosion's eventual separation of trademark/project identity from purely commercial company ownership.

## Purpose Foundation veto-share model

Purpose Foundation documents a steward-ownership model in which a Foundation-held veto share protects defined structural principles without giving the Foundation ordinary operational control.

This demonstrates the possibility of narrowly scoped protective rights.

## Carlsberg Foundation

The Carlsberg Foundation provides a strong real-world example of separating equity ownership from voting control.

Its charter preserves controlling voting rights in Carlsberg A/S even though the Foundation owns a much smaller percentage of the company's economic capital.

This supports Evrosion's post-founder principle:

> economic ownership may be flexible while governance control remains protected.

## EU multiple-vote shares

Directive (EU) 2024/2810 recognizes multiple-vote shares as a control-enhancing structure that can allow controlling shareholders to retain decision-making power while raising outside capital, subject to national law and safeguards.

This supports the feasibility of preserving founder control while allowing limited founder-era outside investment.

# 41. Locked R-002 Laws

The following are accepted governance laws:

1. **The active founder has final control over Evrosion.**
2. **The Foundation is planned now but created later when the project has sufficient scale.**
3. **The founder initially owns/controls Evrosion identity and chooses the first Foundation board.**
4. **Founder control ends only through voluntary retirement/resignation, death, or tightly defined permanent incapacity.**
5. **A successor never inherits founder-level absolute authority.**
6. **Founder succession prefers a founder-designated successor; the Foundation chooses if none exists.**
7. **Post-founder Foundation governance uses a small mixed board, preferably 5–7 and no more than approximately 10.**
8. **No single constituency may dominate the post-founder Foundation.**
9. **Eligible users participate formally in major post-founder constitutional decisions.**
10. **User voting is context-dependent and may use different relevance weights depending on the decision.**
11. **Voting principles must be auditable; anti-Sybil mechanisms may remain partially confidential.**
12. **Voting rules/weights freeze before a ballot opens.**
13. **Evrosion Systems employment grants no automatic user-voting privilege.**
14. **During the founder era, investors cannot remove or overrule the founder.**
15. **Founder-era outside equity is initially intended to remain at roughly 20% maximum if outside investment is used at all.**
16. **Post-founder equity percentages may change freely without transferring project governance away from the Foundation.**
17. **Founder economic ownership may pass to family; founder governance authority does not.**
18. **Post-founder economic ownership and governance authority are deliberately separated.**
19. **The Foundation must possess legally enforceable governance rights over Evrosion Systems after the founder era.**
20. **The post-founder Foundation appoints/removes the Evrosion Systems CEO.**
21. **The CEO controls day-to-day business but cannot unilaterally alter protected constitutional matters.**
22. **Trademark/core identity transfer requires unanimous Foundation-board approval plus overwhelming eligible-user approval.**
23. **Constitutional changes require large Foundation-board and user supermajorities.**
24. **Foundation governance rules cannot be casually self-amended; high board + user approval is required.**
25. **Post-founder emergency powers exist but are narrow, temporary, reviewable, and incapable of permanently rewriting the constitution.**
26. **Founder-era emergency authority remains with the founder.**
27. **Funding, investment, or commercial importance does not automatically grant technical or constitutional authority.**
28. **Exact legal implementation remains replaceable so long as these governance semantics are preserved.**

# 42. Final Governance Model

Conceptually:

```text
                      FOUNDER ERA

                         FOUNDER
                   final project control
                         │
              ┌──────────┴──────────┐
              │                     │
              ▼                     ▼
       EVROSION SYSTEMS       FUTURE FOUNDATION
       commercial engine      stewardship structure
              │                     │
              │                 founder-selected
              │                 initial board
              │
              └──── founder retains final authority ────┘
```

After succession:

```text
                    POST-FOUNDER ERA

                 EVROSION FOUNDATION
                 constitutional steward
                         │
             ┌───────────┼───────────┐
             │           │           │
             ▼           ▼           ▼
       Foundation     Eligible    Protected project
         Board         Users        assets/rules
             │
             │ protected governance rights
             ▼
       EVROSION SYSTEMS
             │
             ├── CEO appointed/removed by Foundation
             ├── day-to-day business
             ├── OEM / enterprise / marketplace / AI
             └── economic shareholders
                    │
                    ├── family
                    ├── investors
                    └── other future owners

Economic ownership may move.
Constitutional governance remains protected.
```

---

# 43. Final Principle

> **The founder builds and leads Evrosion with full authority. The founder's family may inherit the economic value created by that work. But after the founder era, no heir, CEO, investor, corporation, board faction, or single individual inherits ownership of Evrosion's soul.**

The Foundation exists so Evrosion can continue to evolve without becoming easy to capture.
